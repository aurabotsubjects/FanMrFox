# 🦊 FANTASTIC MR FOX — BOOK STUDY UNIT PLAN
### Based on *Fantastic Mr Fox* by Roald Dahl (18 short chapters). Built on the same blueprint as the Wild Robot book study app — same 10-week format, printables, rubrics, lesson plans and teacher/student packs.

---

## 1. HOW TO USE THIS DOCUMENT
This is the complete written version of the content inside the app (`data.js`). Use it to plan, to print sections, or to check answers away from a screen. The app itself is the interactive version: slideshow reads, group worksheets, auto-marked quizzes, project menus, rubric, printable Student Workbook and Teacher Pack.

**Unit shape (one deliberate change from the longer novels):** *Fantastic Mr Fox* is a short book, so each week covers **two chapters** across four reading days — the class hears the chapter read aloud first (Monday / Wednesday, Teacher Read) and then re-reads it in groups with a closer focus on language and inference (Tuesday / Thursday, Group Read). Week 10 has no new reading.

---

## 2. CURRICULUM ALIGNMENT
**Curriculum Alignment — Level 3–4 English, Years 5–6, NZ Curriculum**

**Listening, Reading & Viewing (LRV)**
- Ideas: Show understanding of ideas within, across, and beyond texts.
- Structure and Language: Show understanding of how texts are shaped for different purposes and audiences.
- Processes and Strategies: Integrate sources of information, processes, and strategies confidently to identify, form, and express ideas.

**Speaking, Writing & Presenting (SWP)**
- Ideas: Select, form, and communicate ideas on a range of topics.
- Structure and Language: Show understanding of language features, using them appropriately.
- Processes and Strategies: Select and use processes and strategies to communicate confidently.

**Key Competencies:** Relating to Others, Thinking, Managing Self, Participating & Contributing

**Values:** Cleverness and problem-solving, courage and perseverance, family loyalty, sharing and community — and the moral question of taking what you need versus doing what is right.

**Cross-curricular links:** Science — Living World (burrowing animals and their adaptations, the fox's sense of smell, food chains, wildlife and farming), Technology (machines versus muscle: mechanical shovels and tractors), Maths (108 farm hands, 29 animals at the feast — addition, multiplication and problem solving) and Social Sciences (rich and poor, sharing resources, community).

## 5. DETAILED WEEKLY CONTENT

### WEEK 1 — Chapters 1–2: "Three Farmers and a Fox"
**Learning Intention:** We are learning to work out what a character is like from the words an author chooses to describe them.
**Success Criteria:** I can pick out words and phrases that describe a character, explain what they tell me, and decide what I think of that character.
**Rubric focus:** R1, R2, R8

**Monday — Teacher Read — Chapter 1: The Three Farmers — Boggis, Bunce and Bean**
- **Lead-in:** Think of a character from a book or movie that you really disliked. What did the author or director do to make you feel that way?
- **Question 1 (comprehension):** Name the three farmers and say what kind of animals or crops each one farms.
- **Question 2 (inference):** The author never says Bean is dangerous, but he calls him ‘the cleverest of them all’. Why might that be more worrying than if Bean were just mean?
- **Go-away reflection:** Invent a fourth nasty farmer for the valley. Write two sentences describing what he looks like and what he eats, in the style of Chapter 1.

**Tuesday — Group Read — Chapter 1: Close reading: how Dahl builds his villains**
- **Lead-in:** Have you ever heard a rhyme or chant that people sing about someone else? What was it like — friendly or unkind?
- **Question 1 (comprehension):** Which words and phrases does the author use to describe how each farmer looks and what he eats? List at least two for each farmer.
- **Question 2 (inference):** Why do you think the author describes the farmers by what they eat rather than by what they do? What effect does it have on the reader?
- **Go-away reflection:** Write your own four-line rhyme that the children of the valley might sing about the three farmers. Try to match the rhythm of ‘One fat, one short, one lean’.

**Wednesday — Teacher Read — Chapter 2: Mr Fox — the fox above the valley**
- **Lead-in:** If your family needed something every day, and the only place to get it was guarded, how would you go about it?
- **Question 1 (comprehension):** Where does the Fox family live, and what does Mr Fox do every evening once it gets dark?
- **Question 2 (inference):** How does Mr Fox stay one step ahead of the farmers, and what does this tell you about his character?
- **Go-away reflection:** Draw or describe the Foxes' home as you imagine it, using the clues in the chapter (‘on a hill… in the wood… under the tree’).

**Thursday — Group Read — Chapter 2: Close reading: who is winning the battle of wits?**
- **Lead-in:** Which is more important to you — being strong or being clever? Why?
- **Question 1 (comprehension):** Read Bean's plan again. What does he say the farmers will do tomorrow night, and what has he already found?
- **Question 2 (inference):** Compare Mr Fox and Bean. What do they have in common, and how are they different? Give evidence from the chapter.
- **Go-away reflection:** Write a short diary entry by Mrs Fox at the end of this chapter. She hasn't heard Bean's plan, but what might she be worrying about?

**Project menu**
- **Poster — Wanted: Boggis, Bunce & Bean:** Design a ‘WANTED’ poster from the animals' point of view, listing the three farmers' crimes, their habits and how to spot them from a distance.
- **Comic — A Night at the Farm:** Draw 6 panels showing Mr Fox sniffing the wind and choosing which farm to visit tonight, and how he avoids the waiting farmer.
- **Drama — The Farmers' Meeting:** Act out the conversation between Boggis, Bunce and Bean as they plan to catch the fox — show each farmer's personality through his voice.
- **Journal — Mr Fox's First Log:** Write Mr Fox's diary entry from the start of the story, in his confident, slightly boastful voice, about his clever nightly routine.

**Friday quiz (with answer key)**
1. What are the names of the three farmers?
   - *Answer:* Boggis, Bunce and Bean.
2. What kind of farmer is Boggis?
   - *Answer:* A chicken farmer.
3. What does Bunce farm?
   - *Answer:* Ducks and geese.
4. What does Bean farm?
   - *Answer:* Turkeys and apples (he makes cider from the apples).
5. What does Boggis eat for breakfast, lunch and supper?
   - *Answer:* Three boiled chickens smothered with dumplings.
6. What does Bunce put inside his doughnuts?
   - *Answer:* A paste made from mashed goose-livers.
7. What does Bean drink instead of eating?
   - *Answer:* Gallons of strong cider made from his apples.
8. Which of the three farmers is described as ‘the cleverest of them all’?
   - *Answer:* Bean.
9. True or False: Bunce is described as very tall. *(True/False)*
   - *Answer:* False — he is a pot-bellied dwarf, so short his chin would be underwater in the shallow end of any pool.
10. What is the name of the rhyme the children sing about the farmers? Complete the first line: ‘Boggis and Bunce and…’
   - *Answer:* ‘Boggis and Bunce and Bean / One fat, one short, one lean.’ (accept ‘Bean’)
11. Where does Mr Fox's family live?
   - *Answer:* In a hole under a huge tree in a wood on a hill above the valley.
12. How many Small Foxes are there?
   - *Answer:* Four.
13. What does Mr Fox ask Mrs Fox each evening when it gets dark?
   - *Answer:* What she would like to eat — a chicken from Boggis, a duck or goose from Bunce, or a turkey from Bean.
14. True or False: The farmers are happy that Mr Fox is taking their animals. *(True/False)*
   - *Answer:* False — it makes them wild with rage.
15. How does Mr Fox know when a farmer is hiding near a farm?
   - *Answer:* He approaches with the wind blowing in his face so it carries the farmer's smell to his nose.
16. Vocabulary: What does ‘lurking’ mean?
   - *Answer:* Hiding and waiting, usually with bad intentions.
17. What does Bean say he has found?
   - *Answer:* The fox's hole (up in the wood, under a huge tree).
18. Why do you think the author describes the farmers as ‘fat, short and lean’ and gives them a rhyme?
   - *Answer:* Open-ended — it makes them memorable and slightly ridiculous, so readers dislike them and find them funny.
19. Whose side is the author on in these chapters? How can you tell?
   - *Answer:* Open-ended — the foxes' side; the farmers are described as nasty and mean, while Mr Fox is clever and handsome.
20. Predict: What do you think Bean's plan will mean for the Fox family?
   - *Answer:* Open-ended, text-supported prediction (accept references to an ambush or trap at the hole).

### WEEK 2 — Chapters 3–4: "A Fox Can Dig Quicker Than a Man"
**Learning Intention:** We are learning to notice how authors build suspense with sound words, short sentences and pacing.
**Success Criteria:** I can find the techniques an author uses to build suspense and explain how they make me feel while I'm reading.
**Rubric focus:** R2, R3, R8

**Monday — Teacher Read — Chapter 3: The Shooting — Mr Fox is ambushed**
- **Lead-in:** Have you ever had a feeling that something was about to go wrong? What made you feel that way?
- **Question 1 (comprehension):** What does Mr Fox do at the mouth of his hole before he comes out, and what does he see that makes him jump back?
- **Question 2 (inference):** Mr Fox is described as ‘cocky’ at the start of this chapter. How does this help the ambush feel more surprising and dangerous?
- **Go-away reflection:** Write a two-sentence newspaper headline and first line reporting the shooting from the point of view of the farmers.

**Tuesday — Group Read — Chapter 3: Close reading: creeping, sniffing and freezing**
- **Lead-in:** When you're playing hide and seek, how do you keep really still? What do you notice most — sounds, smells or movements?
- **Question 1 (comprehension):** Find three verbs or phrases that show how slowly and carefully Mr Fox moves out of the hole. What do they have in common?
- **Question 2 (inference):** How does the author make the reader feel the tension before the guns go off? Think about the way sentences are written and what Mr Fox senses.
- **Go-away reflection:** Rewrite the moment Mr Fox sees the gun barrel. Use short sentences and sound words to make it even more tense.

**Wednesday — Teacher Read — Chapter 4: The Terrible Shovels — the foxes dig for their lives**
- **Lead-in:** Imagine you have to make a big decision in only a few seconds because of danger. How would you keep calm?
- **Question 1 (comprehension):** What is the most frightening noise a fox can ever hear, and how does Mr Fox react when he realises what it is?
- **Question 2 (inference):** The Small Fox asks, ‘Will there be dogs?’ What does this question tell you about how the children are feeling?
- **Go-away reflection:** Write three words Mr Fox might whisper to his family in the tunnel to keep them going. Explain why you chose them.

**Thursday — Group Read — Chapter 4: Close reading: ‘Scrunch, scrunch, scrunch’**
- **Lead-in:** What is your favourite sound word (like ‘crash’ or ‘splash’)? Why does it work so well?
- **Question 1 (comprehension):** Where in Chapter 4 does the author use sound words, and what are they describing?
- **Question 2 (inference):** Mrs Fox says, ‘Your father is a fantastic fox.’ What has Mr Fox done to earn this? Use two details from the chapter.
- **Go-away reflection:** Make a short list of the adjectives you would use to describe Mr Fox after reading these two chapters. Choose one and explain it.

**Project menu**
- **Poster — Beware: Farmers with Guns!:** Create a warning poster for the wood, telling the animals how the farmers set their trap and how to smell them out.
- **Comic — The Ambush:** Show the moment of the ambush in 6 panels: Mr Fox poking his nose out, the glint, the jump, the guns and the tail.
- **Drama — Digging for Our Lives:** In a small group, act out the family digging deeper and deeper. Use voices, sound effects and movement to show the shovels getting closer.
- **Journal — A Very Short Tail:** Write Mr Fox's diary entry that evening, describing how it felt to lose his tail and what Mrs Fox said to him.

**Friday quiz (with answer key)**
1. What does Mrs Fox ask Mr Fox to bring for supper?
   - *Answer:* Two fat ducks (one for them, one for the children).
2. From which farmer does Mr Fox plan to take the ducks?
   - *Answer:* Bunce.
3. What does Mr Fox say each farmer smells of? Name one.
   - *Answer:* Boggis: rotten chicken-skins; Bunce: goose-livers; Bean: cider fumes.
4. Where are the three farmers waiting?
   - *Answer:* Outside the entrance to the fox's hole, each crouching behind a tree with a loaded gun.
5. Why can't Mr Fox smell the farmers this time?
   - *Answer:* They chose their positions carefully so the wind blew from the fox's hole towards them, carrying their smell away from Mr Fox.
6. What tiny noise does Mr Fox hear?
   - *Answer:* A soft rustling, as though someone had moved a foot through dry leaves.
7. What does Mr Fox tell himself the noise probably was?
   - *Answer:* A field-mouse or some other small animal.
8. What bright thing does Mr Fox see behind a tree?
   - *Answer:* The barrel of a gun with a speck of moonlight on it.
9. What do the farmers find lying at the hole?
   - *Answer:* Mr Fox's tail — shot off.
10. True or False: The farmers shot Mr Fox dead. *(True/False)*
   - *Answer:* False — they got the tail but missed the fox.
11. What does Bean suggest they do once they realise the fox has escaped down the hole?
   - *Answer:* Dig him out (Get the shovels!).
12. Vocabulary: What does ‘cocky’ mean?
   - *Answer:* Overly confident or boastful.
13. Who is licking Mr Fox's tail stump to stop the bleeding?
   - *Answer:* Mrs Fox.
14. What noise wakes Mr Fox up?
   - *Answer:* The scrape-scrape-scraping of shovels digging into the soil.
15. What does Mr Fox realise that ‘nobody in the world’ can do?
   - *Answer:* Dig as quickly as a fox (a fox can dig quicker than a man).
16. Which direction does Mr Fox tell everyone to dig?
   - *Answer:* Downwards — as deep as possible.
17. True or False: The whole family, including the Small Foxes, helps to dig. *(True/False)*
   - *Answer:* True.
18. How long do they dig before Mr Fox says ‘Hold it!’?
   - *Answer:* About an hour.
19. What does Mrs Fox say about her husband at the end of Chapter 4?
   - *Answer:* That if it wasn't for him they would all be dead — he is a fantastic fox.
20. Why do you think the author has the farmers use ‘the most frightening noise a fox can ever hear’ to introduce the shovels?
   - *Answer:* Open-ended — it builds fear and suspense before the action starts; accept reasoned answers.

### WEEK 3 — Chapters 5–6: "Machines Against Foxes"
**Learning Intention:** We are learning to explain how an author shows time passing and danger increasing across a scene.
**Success Criteria:** I can explain how the setting and the events change through a scene, and what effect these changes have on the characters and on me as a reader.
**Rubric focus:** R2, R3, R8

**Monday — Teacher Read — Chapter 5: The Terrible Tractors — Bean's mechanical shovels**
- **Lead-in:** Have you ever seen a huge digger or a bulldozer at work? What was it like — how big, how loud?
- **Question 1 (comprehension):** What does Bean suggest when the farmers can't dig the foxes out by hand, and what do Bean and Bunce fetch?
- **Question 2 (inference):** The farmers are tired and cross and arguing with each other. Why might this make them even more dangerous to the foxes?
- **Go-away reflection:** Describe the sights and sounds of the tractors arriving in the wood. Use at least three descriptive words.

**Tuesday — Group Read — Chapter 5: Close reading: monsters, matchsticks and murderous machines**
- **Lead-in:** What is the noisiest place you've ever been? How did it feel?
- **Question 1 (comprehension):** Find two comparisons or descriptions the author uses to make the tractors seem huge and frightening. What do they compare the machines to?
- **Question 2 (inference):** Bean is described as deaf because of the dirt in his ears. Why do you think the author includes such an unpleasant, funny detail?
- **Go-away reflection:** Write three sentences from the Small Foxes' point of view, hearing the machines above their heads for the first time.

**Wednesday — Teacher Read — Chapter 6: The Race — the machines against the foxes**
- **Lead-in:** What is the longest you have ever kept going at something really hard? What kept you going?
- **Question 1 (comprehension):** What happens to the hill as the day goes on, and what do the foxes hear each time the machines get closer?
- **Question 2 (inference):** The author includes small pictures of how the hill looks at different times of day. Why might he have done this instead of just using words?
- **Go-away reflection:** Choose one moment from the race. Write what Mr Fox says to encourage his family.

**Thursday — Group Read — Chapter 6: Close reading: madness, mockery and crowds**
- **Lead-in:** What do you think makes some people refuse to give up, even when everyone tells them they're wrong?
- **Question 1 (comprehension):** What do the crowds from the villages say and do when they come to look at the crater?
- **Question 2 (inference):** The text says ‘a sort of madness had taken hold of the three men’. What evidence supports this? Give at least two examples.
- **Go-away reflection:** Design a caption for a photograph of the crater taken from above, as it might appear in a local newspaper.

**Project menu**
- **Poster — The Hill Through Time:** Create a three-stage poster showing the hill at the start, at lunchtime and at five o'clock, using colour and size to show how the hill is destroyed.
- **Comic — The Race:** Draw a 6-panel action comic of the foxes digging while the shovel edge scrapes just behind them. Use sound words in the panels.
- **Drama — Voices from the Crater:** Act out the crowd looking down into the crater — the farmers, the jeering villagers and (if you dare) the foxes below.
- **Journal — The Foreman's Diary:** Write a diary entry by one of the farmers at the end of Day 2 — do they feel like winners or fools? Show their point of view.

**Friday quiz (with answer key)**
1. What time of day do the farmers give up digging by hand?
   - *Answer:* Sunrise — the next morning, after digging all night.
2. How deep was the hole the farmers had dug?
   - *Answer:* So deep you could have put a house into it.
3. Who does Bunce blame for the idea of digging?
   - *Answer:* Bean.
4. Why can Bean not hear properly?
   - *Answer:* His earholes are clogged with muck, wax, chewing gum and dead flies.
5. What does Bean say they need to catch the foxes?
   - *Answer:* Machines — mechanical shovels.
6. Who stays behind to guard the hole with a gun?
   - *Answer:* Boggis.
7. What are the machines that Bean and Bunce bring back?
   - *Answer:* Two enormous black caterpillar tractors with mechanical shovels.
8. What happens to the huge tree above the fox's hole?
   - *Answer:* It is toppled like a matchstick.
9. What does one of the Small Foxes notice about the tunnel?
   - *Answer:* It has got shorter — they can see daylight.
10. What does Mr Fox shout at the end of Chapter 5?
   - *Answer:* ‘Dig for your lives! Dig, dig, dig!’
11. True or False: The tractors are described as gentle machines. *(True/False)*
   - *Answer:* False — they are murderous, brutal-looking monsters.
12. Vocabulary: What does ‘obstinate’ mean?
   - *Answer:* Stubborn; refusing to change your mind or give up.
13. What does Chapter 6 call the struggle between the machines and the foxes?
   - *Answer:* A desperate race.
14. What do the farmers refuse to do because they are so keen to finish the job?
   - *Answer:* Stop for lunch.
15. What is the crater compared to in Chapter 6?
   - *Answer:* The crater of a volcano.
16. What did crowds from the villages do when they saw the crater?
   - *Answer:* Came to look at it and laughed at and jeered at the farmers (‘You must be mad!’).
17. True or False: The jeering crowd makes the farmers give up. *(True/False)*
   - *Answer:* False — it only makes them more furious and obstinate.
18. How do the little pictures of the hill (in the book) help you understand the story?
   - *Answer:* Open-ended — they show at a glance how much of the hill has been dug away and how much time has passed.
19. Why do you think the author has the farmers dig even after the whole village is laughing at them?
   - *Answer:* Open-ended — accept ideas about pride, stubbornness and obsession.
20. Predict: What will the farmers do next if the machines still haven't caught the foxes?
   - *Answer:* Open-ended, text-supported prediction (accept ‘wait them out’, ‘starve them’, etc.).

### WEEK 4 — Chapters 7–8: "We'll Never Let Him Go"
**Learning Intention:** We are learning to compare characters' points of view and explain what motivates their actions.
**Success Criteria:** I can explain what a character wants and why, using evidence, and compare two very different points of view in the same scene.
**Rubric focus:** R1, R7, R9

**Monday — Teacher Read — Chapter 7: ‘We'll Never Let Him Go’ — the farmers make an oath**
- **Lead-in:** Have you ever refused to give up on something even when it was clearly not working? What made you keep going?
- **Question 1 (comprehension):** What do the three farmers swear to do, and how do they seal their promise?
- **Question 2 (inference):** Bean smiles ‘a sickly smile’ when he suggests starving the fox out. What does this tell you about his character and feelings?
- **Go-away reflection:** Write the words of the farmers' solemn oath as you imagine they said it.

**Tuesday — Group Read — Chapter 7: Close reading: rage, cowardice and cunning**
- **Lead-in:** What is the difference between being determined and being stubborn? Which are the farmers?
- **Question 1 (comprehension):** Find two moments when the farmers disagree or argue with each other. What does each argument show about them?
- **Question 2 (inference):** Bunce shouts ‘Not me!’ when Bean says he should go down the hole. What does this reveal about Bunce, and why might the author have included it?
- **Go-away reflection:** Write a short conversation between two villagers on the crater's edge discussing whether the farmers have gone completely mad.

**Wednesday — Teacher Read — Chapter 8: The Foxes Begin to Starve — the siege**
- **Lead-in:** How do you feel when you're really hungry and can smell food but can't eat it? Describe it.
- **Question 1 (comprehension):** What do the three farmers eat while they wait, and what does Boggis do to try to tempt the foxes?
- **Question 2 (inference):** Mrs Fox says ‘That's just what they want you to do.’ What does she understand that the Small Foxes don't?
- **Go-away reflection:** Imagine you are one of the Small Foxes in the tunnel. Write your feelings in four sentences.

**Thursday — Group Read — Chapter 8: Close reading: a hundred and eight men with guns**
- **Lead-in:** Have you ever been trapped inside and unable to go out? How did it feel after the first few hours?
- **Question 1 (comprehension):** How many men do the farmers order to surround the hill? Show how you would work this out from the numbers in the text.
- **Question 2 (inference):** Boggis asks about digging through the hill and Bean ‘pretends’ he thought of it. What does this show about Bean, and how does it make the story funnier?
- **Go-away reflection:** Compare life above ground for the farmers and life below ground for the foxes at the end of this chapter. Show it as a two-column list.

**Project menu**
- **Poster — Trapped: The Siege of the Hill:** Design a cutaway poster showing the farmers' camp on top and the foxes' tunnel below, with labels showing what each side is doing, feeling and eating.
- **Comic — The Smell of Chicken:** Show the temptation scene in 6 panels: Boggis waving a chicken at the hole, the hungry Small Foxes, and Mrs Fox's firm reply.
- **Drama — The Solemn Oath:** Act out the three farmers swearing their oath in the crater. How can voices and body language show their obsession?
- **Journal — A Night on the Hill:** Write a diary entry by Mrs Fox about the first night of the siege. What is she worried about? What does she hope?

**Friday quiz (with answer key)**
1. At what time do Bean and Bunce switch off their tractors?
   - *Answer:* Six o'clock in the evening.
2. Where do the three farmers walk to after switching off the machines?
   - *Answer:* The small fox's hole at the bottom of the huge crater.
3. What do the three farmers swear an oath about?
   - *Answer:* They will not go back to their farms until the fox is caught.
4. Who refuses to climb down the hole to fetch the fox?
   - *Answer:* Bunce (‘Not me!’).
5. What does Bean say they should do instead?
   - *Answer:* Starve the fox out by camping day and night watching the hole.
6. What do the farmers send to their farms for?
   - *Answer:* Tents, sleeping-bags and supper.
7. What do Boggis, Bunce and Bean each eat and drink for supper in the crater?
   - *Answer:* Boggis: three boiled chickens with dumplings; Bunce: six doughnuts with goose-liver paste; Bean: two gallons of cider.
8. How does Boggis tempt the foxes?
   - *Answer:* He holds a steaming chicken near the hole so the smell wafts down the tunnel.
9. Why does Mrs Fox forbid the Small Foxes from sneaking up to grab the chicken?
   - *Answer:* It's a trap — that's exactly what the farmers want them to do.
10. What do Bunce and Bean use to light the hole at night?
   - *Answer:* The powerful headlamps of the two tractors.
11. True or False: The farmers take turns to keep watch. *(True/False)*
   - *Answer:* True — one watches while two sleep.
12. How many men work on Boggis's farm? On Bunce's? On Bean's?
   - *Answer:* 35, 36 and 37.
13. How many men are there altogether, and what will they do?
   - *Answer:* 108 (35 + 36 + 37); they will form a ring around the hill armed with guns and flashlights to stop the fox escaping.
14. True or False: The farmers cut off every possible escape for the foxes. *(True/False)*
   - *Answer:* True — a tight ring of armed men makes it impossible for any animal to escape.
15. How does Mr Fox check whether the farmers are still outside?
   - *Answer:* He creeps to the mouth of the tunnel and sniffs — he can smell Bean a mile away.
16. Vocabulary: What does ‘solemn’ mean?
   - *Answer:* Serious and formal.
17. Why might the author have made the farmers so greedy that they eat while the foxes starve?
   - *Answer:* Open-ended — it contrasts the farmers' greed and cruelty with the foxes' suffering, so readers side with the foxes.
18. Boggis asks, ‘What if the fox digs a hole right through the hill?’ Why is this a clever question?
   - *Answer:* It shows Boggis can think ahead; it also sets up the fact that this is exactly what Mr Fox will do.
19. How is Mrs Fox's response to hunger different from the Small Foxes'?
   - *Answer:* Open-ended — she thinks ahead and stays calm to protect them; they act on impulse.
20. Predict: What might Mr Fox do next to escape from the siege?
   - *Answer:* Open-ended, text-supported prediction (accept ideas about digging a new tunnel).

### WEEK 5 — Chapters 9–10: "Mr Fox Has a Plan"
**Learning Intention:** We are learning to make predictions from clues and then check them against what the author reveals.
**Success Criteria:** I can make a prediction using clues from the text, explain my reasoning and check whether I was right.
**Rubric focus:** R3, R8, R4

**Monday — Teacher Read — Chapter 9: Mr Fox Has a Plan — three days and nights**
- **Lead-in:** Have you ever had an idea that seemed too good to be true? What did you do with it?
- **Question 1 (comprehension):** How long has the siege lasted, and what are the Small Foxes asking their parents?

- **Extended Drama/Performance:** Dramatise a moment from the story — or a scene after the last page, such as the farmers finally giving up — with sound effects, costumes and a narrator.
- **Character Case File — Mr Fox:** An in-depth profile (poster + written report + panels) tracking Mr Fox's cleverness and courage across the whole book, with evidence from every part of the story.
- **"Our Underground Village" Class Anthology:** Each student contributes one illustrated page: a house, shop or community area in the underground village, with a short description of how the animals share and help each other.

### 3-lesson structure (fixed)
- **Monday — Plan:** Choose project, plan/storyboard, gather resources, assign group roles.
- **Tuesday — Produce:** Main production time.
- **Wednesday — Polish:** Finishing touches, rehearsal/polish, prepare for presenting.

### Thursday
Presentation / Gallery Walk Day — students present or display their final projects. Peer feedback via "Glow & Grow" sticky notes (one thing that shone, one thing to consider next time).

### Friday
Celebration & Whole-Unit Reflection — no quiz this week. Whole-class reflection circle: share the one big idea students will carry forward from this book. Certificates presented. Optional: hold a class ‘Fox's Feast’ — a shared picnic with everyone bringing something to share.

### Final project rubric
| Criteria | Strand | Not Achieved | Working Towards | Achieved | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Understanding of the text | R1 | Retells a few basic plot events. | Explains some of Mr Fox's actions or motivations, with limited evidence. | Explains a theme (cleverness, courage, community) and supports it with clear evidence from the book. | A Character Case File showing Mr Fox being clever at the start (the wind), brave in the middle (the race) and generous at the end (the feast), with an event from each. |
| Communication of ideas | R4 · R5 | Ideas are unclear or unfinished. | Ideas are mostly clear but not always organised for the audience. | Ideas are clear, organised and suit the audience and purpose. | A Clever Fox campaign poster with a catchy slogan, three persuasive reasons and a picture that matches the message. |
| Creativity & effort | R6 | Minimal effort or detail; work is incomplete. | Some effort shown; parts are rushed or unfinished. | Finished work showing care, original ideas and improvements made after feedback. | A drama that adds a new scene after the last page, rehearsed and improved after a Glow & Grow from another group. |
| Collaboration (if group) | R7 · R9 | Limited teamwork; relies on others to do the work. | Works with others when prompted; does their own part. | Shares the work fairly, listens to others and helps the group succeed. | Each member has a clear role, the group checks in with each other, and everyone presents a part. |

## 7. READING PROGRESS RUBRIC & MARKING GUIDE
Three levels: **Not Achieved · Working Towards · Achieved** (Achieved = the Level 3 curriculum expectation has been met). Judge the student's usual, consistent work rather than a one-off.

### R1 — Ideas (Listening, Reading & Viewing)
- **Curriculum:** Show a developing understanding of ideas within, across, and beyond texts.
- **Expect:** Explains the big ideas of the story (cleverness and problem-solving, courage, family loyalty, sharing and community) and supports them with events from the book, making links beyond the text to self or the world.
- **Student "I can":** I can explain a big idea from the story and back it up with something that happened in the book.
- **Quick question when marking:** Does the student explain WHY an event matters — not just WHAT happened?
- **Not Achieved:** Retells some events but cannot yet explain what they mean. Answers are mostly 'what happened'.
- **Working Towards:** Names a big idea (e.g. 'Mr Fox is clever') with a general reason, but the link to the book is vague or missing. *Sample:* The book is about a clever fox because he tricks the farmers.
- **Achieved:** Explains a big idea and supports it with a specific event from the book. Makes a link beyond the text (to self or the world).
- **Achieved exemplar:** I think the biggest idea is that being clever and being generous is stronger than being big and mean. The farmers have guns and tractors, but Mr Fox uses his brain and his digging skills, and then he shares the food with Badger, Mole, Rabbit and Weasel so everyone survives. It reminds me of how our class works best when we help each other out instead of competing.
- **Where to find evidence:** Comprehension & inference answers, Friday quizzes, go-away reflections

### R2 — Structure & Language (Listening, Reading & Viewing)
- **Curriculum:** Show a developing understanding of how texts are shaped for different purposes and audiences, and how language features are used for effect.
- **Expect:** Notices the choices the author makes — short chapters that end on cliffhangers, sound words like ‘scrunch, scrunch, scrunch’, rhyme and rhythm in the songs, humorous exaggeration in the farmers' descriptions, the narrator's voice — and explains how they affect the reader.
- **Student "I can":** I can notice how the author wrote the book and explain how it makes the reader think or feel.
- **Quick question when marking:** Does the student name a writer's choice AND say what effect it has on the reader?
- **Not Achieved:** Comments only on the plot; does not yet notice how the book is written.
- **Working Towards:** Notices a feature (e.g. 'there are sound words') but cannot explain its effect. *Sample:* The chapters are really short and there are funny words.
- **Achieved:** Names a writer's choice and explains its effect on the reader, using an example from the book.
- **Achieved exemplar:** When the shovels come through the ceiling, Roald Dahl uses sound words like ‘scrunch, scrunch, scrunch’ and very short sentences. This made me feel the foxes' fear because it sounded like the danger was right on top of them. Ending the chapter on a good moment made me want to keep reading to see what happened next.
- **Where to find evidence:** Language and structure questions (esp. Weeks with R2), Tuesday & Thursday close-reading answers, class discussion

### R3 — Processes & Strategies (Listening, Reading & Viewing)
- **Curriculum:** Integrate sources of information, processes, and strategies with developing confidence to identify, form, and express ideas.
- **Expect:** Uses reading strategies — predicting, questioning, making connections, summarising — and links earlier chapters to later ones (cause and effect across the book).
- **Student "I can":** I can use reading strategies and link what happened earlier in the book to what happens later.
- **Quick question when marking:** Can the student connect an earlier event to a later one without being told?
- **Not Achieved:** Relies on the teacher to explain the story; does not yet connect chapters.
- **Working Towards:** Uses a strategy when prompted (e.g. predicts when asked) and makes simple links between chapters. *Sample:* Mr Fox digs a tunnel and then he gets to the chicken house.
- **Achieved:** Independently uses strategies and explains how an earlier event causes or sets up a later one.
- **Achieved exemplar:** In Chapter 2 we learn that Mr Fox always approaches a farm knowing exactly where the buildings are. That's why in Chapter 10 he can dig straight to Boggis's Chicken House Number One first time. I predicted he was heading for the farms as soon as he said he knew a place that was ‘marvellous’.
- **Where to find evidence:** Predictions, cause-and-effect questions, weekly quizzes (review questions)

### R4 — Ideas (Speaking, Writing & Presenting)
- **Curriculum:** Select, form, and communicate ideas on a range of topics.
- **Expect:** Writes and shares clear, developed ideas about the book — opinions, explanations and reflections — with supporting detail.
- **Student "I can":** I can share a clear idea or opinion and give reasons to support it.
- **Quick question when marking:** Is there a clear idea PLUS at least two reasons or details?
- **Not Achieved:** Answers are one word or unclear; ideas are not explained.
- **Working Towards:** Shares a clear idea but gives little detail or only one reason. *Sample:* I think it was okay for Mr Fox to steal because he was hungry.
- **Achieved:** Communicates a clear opinion or idea and develops it with at least two reasons or pieces of evidence.
- **Achieved exemplar:** I think Mr Fox was right to take the food. First, the farmers were trying to kill him and his family, and they had already shot off his tail. Second, he only took a few things so nobody would notice, and he shared the food with the other animals. But I also agree with Badger that stealing isn't perfect, so it's a hard choice.
- **Where to find evidence:** Go-away reflections, journal projects, Week 7 opinion writing

### R5 — Structure & Language (Speaking, Writing & Presenting)
- **Curriculum:** Use language features appropriately, showing a developing understanding of their effects; organise texts using a range of structures.
- **Expect:** Chooses the right structure and language for the task — a wanted poster, a diary in Mr Fox's voice, a comic, a script, a rhyming song.
- **Student "I can":** I can choose the right kind of writing and words for the job (like a diary, a poster or a song).
- **Quick question when marking:** Does the writing look and sound like the form it's meant to be?
- **Not Achieved:** Writing does not match the task; little attempt at structure or language features.
- **Working Towards:** Attempts the right form (e.g. writes a diary) but the language or structure is inconsistent. *Sample:* Dear diary, today the farmers digged and I was scared and then we dug more and I got away.
- **Achieved:** Uses a suitable structure and language features for the purpose and audience (e.g. a boastful, chatty voice for Mr Fox's diary; sound words and rhyme for a song; persuasive language for a wanted poster).
- **Achieved exemplar:** Dear Diary, Tonight I poked my handsome nose out of the hole and — CRACK! — three guns went off at once. I lost my tail, but not my nerve. Mrs Fox says I am fantastic, and I have to admit she is right. Tomorrow the farmers will dig, but nobody, and I mean nobody, digs quicker than a fox.
- **Where to find evidence:** Poster, comic, drama & journal projects; Week 10 final project

### R6 — Processes & Strategies (Speaking, Writing & Presenting)
- **Curriculum:** Integrate sources of information, processes, and strategies with developing confidence to identify, form, and express ideas.
- **Expect:** Plans, drafts, revises and shares work, using feedback to improve it.
- **Student "I can":** I can plan my work, improve it, and use feedback to make it better.
- **Quick question when marking:** Can you see a plan AND a change made because of feedback?
- **Not Achieved:** Needs step-by-step support to start; work is not planned or revised.
- **Working Towards:** Plans and drafts with support; makes small changes when prompted. *Sample:* I finished my comic. My teacher told me to add colour so I did.
- **Achieved:** Independently plans, drafts and revises, and uses feedback (e.g. Glow & Grow) to improve the work.
- **Achieved exemplar:** My plan had three parts: the shovels, the tractors and the escape. My partner's 'grow' said my escape was rushed, so I added two more panels showing the foxes digging sideways towards Boggis's farm. Now the story makes more sense.
- **Where to find evidence:** Project plans, Week 10 planning lesson, Glow & Grow feedback

### R7 — Relating to Others (Key Competency)
- **Curriculum:** Relating to others: listen actively, recognise different points of view, negotiate and share ideas.
- **Expect:** Listens to and builds on others' ideas during group reading and projects, and takes turns fairly.
- **Student "I can":** I can listen to my group, take turns, and build on other people's ideas.
- **Quick question when marking:** Does the student respond to what others say, not just wait for their own turn?
- **Not Achieved:** Rarely listens or takes part in group reading; may interrupt or opt out.
- **Working Towards:** Takes part and takes turns when reminded; sometimes responds to others' ideas. *Sample:* (Observed) Reads their turn when the group reminds them; agrees with others but doesn't add to their ideas.
- **Achieved:** Listens actively, takes turns, and builds on or respectfully challenges others' ideas.
- **Achieved exemplar:** (Observed) "I sort of disagree with Jack — I think Mr Fox knew stealing was risky, because Badger asked him about it and he had to think about his answer." She then asked the quieter group members what they thought.
- **Where to find evidence:** Tuesday & Thursday group reads (turn tracker), drama projects — teacher observation

### R8 — Thinking (Key Competency)
- **Curriculum:** Thinking: use creative, critical and metacognitive processes to make sense of information, experiences and ideas.
- **Expect:** Makes inferences — works out what characters feel and why — using clues from the text, and thinks creatively about possibilities.
- **Student "I can":** I can use clues in the story to work out things the author doesn't tell me.
- **Quick question when marking:** Has the student worked something out AND pointed to the clue?
- **Not Achieved:** Only answers questions where the answer is stated directly in the text.
- **Working Towards:** Makes an inference with prompting but struggles to explain the clue. *Sample:* Mr Fox is worried because the farmers are digging.
- **Achieved:** Independently infers feelings or motives and points to the clue in the text that shows it.
- **Achieved exemplar:** The book never says Mr Fox is afraid, but when the shovel breaks through the ceiling he jumps up shouting and starts digging straight away. That clue tells me he is terrified for his family, but he turns that fear into action instead of freezing.
- **Where to find evidence:** Inference questions (Question 2 every day), quizzes, predictions

### R9 — Participating & Contributing (Key Competency)
- **Curriculum:** Participating and contributing: be actively involved in groups and communities, contributing and taking responsibility.
- **Expect:** Contributes ideas in class discussions and projects, and takes responsibility for a role in group work.
- **Student "I can":** I can share my ideas and do my part in group work.
- **Quick question when marking:** Does the student offer ideas and take on a job without being asked?
- **Not Achieved:** Rarely contributes to discussions or group projects.
- **Working Towards:** Contributes when asked; completes their part of a group task with support. *Sample:* (Observed) Shares an idea in the reflection circle when called on; completes their part of the poster.
- **Achieved:** Offers ideas without being asked, takes responsibility for a role, and encourages others.
- **Achieved exemplar:** (Observed) In the Week 10 drama group, Tane volunteered to be director, made a scene list, and made sure everyone had a part. He shared two ideas in the reflection circle without being asked.
- **Where to find evidence:** Class discussions, project work, Week 10 group project & gallery walk

### R10 — Cleverness, Courage & Community (Values)
- **Curriculum:** Values: excellence, innovation and enquiry, community and participation, integrity, and respect.
- **Expect:** Connects the book's values — clever problem-solving, courage when things are hard, looking after family, and sharing with the wider community — to their own life and actions.
- **Student "I can":** I can connect the book's message about cleverness, courage and community to my own life.
- **Quick question when marking:** Has the student linked a value from the book to something real in their own life?
- **Not Achieved:** Cannot yet name the book's values or connect them to themselves.
- **Working Towards:** Names a value (e.g. 'be brave' or 'share') when asked, with a simple or general link. *Sample:* The book teaches us to share with other people.
- **Achieved:** Explains a value from the book and connects it to a real experience or action of their own.
- **Achieved exemplar:** Mr Fox could have kept the feast for his own family, but he invited Badger, Mole, Rabbit and Weasel because he knew the trouble was his fault. When our netball team lost a player, I gave up my turn on the shooting so the new girl could join in. Like Mr Fox, I learned that when you share, everyone ends up stronger.
- **Where to find evidence:** Go-away reflections, journal entries, Week 10 final project, reflection circle
